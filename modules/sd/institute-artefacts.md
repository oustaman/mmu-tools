All six elements land.

**How the prototype gap was closed.** The hand-in became a **slide deck** —
PowerPoint or Google Slides, one section per slide. Because each of the 6–8
screens is then already its own slide, linking them is six right-clicks, and the
links survive the export to PDF. A new section **B8** records them as a from→to
list, a new rule ("One screen per slide") tells students in week 1 why it matters,
and week 10 spends fifteen minutes doing it with the links checked in the room.
The brief permits "Figma, Adobe XD, Powerpoint/Clickable PDF **or equivalent**";
Adobe XD has been discontinued for years, and PowerPoint/Google Slides is the
equivalent every student already has.

The rubric now says links are **checked for existence and destination, not
elegance** — which removes the marks-for-sloppiness problem without removing the
requirement the approved spec makes.

### SD·01 — Generated screens, measured
`interfaces.institute/collections/generated/` · **BUILT 2026-09-02 — commissioned and empty, on purpose**

> The room and `pipeline/capture_generated.mjs` ship; the rows do not, and may
> not be fabricated — see `datasets/generated/CAPTURE.md`. Deliberately **not**
> registered in `COLLECTIONS`, so the site's collection count stays a claim
> about pages a reader can actually read. Week 6's two items point at it, which
> makes the live demonstration the artefact this term and puts the schema
> somewhere a colleague can find it.

*The original proposal:*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Serves** | Week 6 · syllabus 2 · module A1 + B2 · **programme B6** |
| **Why** | Week 6 is the only week with **zero** institute artefacts — three live demos and a link back to the module's own page. It feeds A7 and C8, both named in the assessment brief, and it is where the example portfolio's central fault sits. It is also the only place the module can serve programme outcome B6, which no other artefact touches. |

**Method.** Fix one prompt set — say six screens of a single product. Run it
through each generator that ships a UI mode. Measure every output against the
same spec sheet the module makes students fill in: contrast ratio on every text
pair, target sizes, type scale ratio, columns/gutter/margin, and which of the
five states are present. Record separately what the tool *claims* and what it
*ships*.

**Must contain**
- One row per generated screen, one column per measured property.
- The prompt, verbatim, and the date and version of each tool.
- Screens drawn or reproduced under the institute's own rules — no screenshots of
  anyone's product chrome.
- A column for *declared vs measured*, which is the whole argument.

**The finding it should be able to state.** Something of the form
*"N of the M generated screens fail 4.5:1 on body text"* · *"N ship no empty
state"* · *"N have a target under 44 px"*. Deliberately parallel to the button
page's *42 of the 63 are shorter than 44 pixels*, so a student meets the same
sentence shape twice.

**Check first.** Whether `/collections/design-tools/` already covers the
measuring instruments; this room should measure *output*, not tools.

### SD·02 — Hierarchy
`interfaces.institute/stairwell/hierarchy/` · **serves two weeks — build this second**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Serves** | **Week 1** · Week 2 · **syllabus 3** · module B1 |
| **Why** | The Stairwell holds ten principles — affordance, constraint, feedback, Fitts, Hick, Jakob, Miller, recognition, state, Tesler — and **none of them is hierarchy**, which is the entire subject of week 2 and a named syllabus item. For a design module that is the odd absence in the building. |
| **And week 1** | Week 1's only gap is *"three live teardowns — what do you read first, and which of five devices did it"*, and week 2's only gap is *"scale, weight, space — live"*. **They are the same missing artefact.** Its takeaway says so outright: *that vocabulary is what C2 is written in* — so this is not only a lecture prop, it is the language of a rationale section every student has to write and every marker has to mark. |

**Method.** Standard Stairwell room: the principle stated plainly, its provenance,
and a bench — *same task, built twice*.

**Must contain**
- A bench: one screen with a single dominant element, one with three competing.
  Task: find and press the primary action. Count wrong clicks, as the affordance
  bench does.
- The five devices that establish dominance — **scale, weight, colour, space,
  position** — each isolated and demonstrable one at a time. This list is fixed by
  the module: week 1 and week 2 both name these five and C2 is written in them.
- **Three worked readings**, which is what week 1 performs live: a news front page,
  a checkout, and one that is genuinely unclear. Each one answers the same two
  questions in the same order — what do you read first, and which of the five did
  it. The third is the one that earns its place; a screen where the answer is
  contested teaches more than two where it is obvious.
- A measured claim off existing data, not an assertion.

**The finding.** Read off the 68 grids or the 50 banks: how many establish a
single dominant element above the fold, and how many present three or more
equal-weight candidates.

**Check first.** Whether the bench registry in `interfaces.institute` will accept
an eleventh principle without a data migration — the Stairwell page throws at
build time if a principle has no bench.

**Free wins for week 1, available today.** The Whole Systems floor has **2 of 9
rooms written and both unused by this module**:
`/whole-systems/bank-navigation/` (HSBC UK × Hong Kong, one interface read in two
markets) and `/whole-systems/mtr-station-colour/` (*the station you can find
without reading* — 98 stations, and the MTR publishes no station colours, so every
value in it was compiled). Either is a teardown already written. They are deeper
and differently shaped than a five-device reading, so they supplement this room
rather than replace it — but they mean week 1 can drop from one gap to none with
two links and one build.

### SD·05 — Real content versus placeholder
`interfaces.institute/collections/flow-census/#placeholder` · **BUILT 2026-09-02**

> **Shipped in a different shape from the proposal below, and the proposal was
> wrong.** The plan was to survey design systems for whether their component
> examples use real content. What was built instead is a live specimen on the
> flow census: the same 300px card rendered three ways — one placeholder in
> every slot, lorem matched word-for-word to each real heading, and the twenty-four
> real headings — with heights, column length and wrap counts measured off the
> rendered page.
>
> The reason for the change is a measurement. The usual claim — that lorem ipsum
> misleads because Latin words are the wrong length — was tested and is false:
> 5.31 characters per word against 5.38 for these headings. Matched word for word,
> lorem behaves as the real thing does (6 of 24 cards wrapping against 5). The
> vice is the set a wireframe actually uses, one string everywhere, where nothing
> wraps at all. A survey of design systems would have counted the wrong thing.
>
> The three-way comparison exists so the middle set is a control. Without it this
> is a plausible claim; with it, it survives someone checking.

*The original proposal, kept for the record:*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Serves** | Week 8 · syllabus 5 |
| **Why** | W8's *"lorem ipsum versus real content"* has no artefact. Small, cheap, and the register already holds the source material. |

**Method.** For each design system in the register, record whether its component
examples use real content or placeholder, and whether its guidance says anything
about the difference.

**The finding.** How many ship components demonstrated only on text that cannot
wrap, break or run long.

### SD·06 — Published user flows
`interfaces.institute/collections/flow-census/` · **BUILT 2026-09-02**

> **Shipped as *The flow census*, from this module's own captures rather than
> from published flow documentation.** Four public services were walked end to
> end and photographed — Register to Vote, NHS Find a GP, a news front page, a
> retail product page — and the collection is the reduction of those 43 captures
> to the 24 screens they contain.
>
> The finding: a form photographed empty and then filled in is one screen, twice.
> Twenty of the twenty-four screens carry a control leading somewhere the walk
> did not go — a postcode you do not know, an address not on the list, a
> nationality you cannot provide. Only four have no way off but forward, and
> three of those take one value and continue. **The happy path is the smallest
> part of a real service**, which is the whole of week 7 in one number.
>
> No screenshots are published and none may be: showing captures of a live
> service to a class is fair dealing for instruction, a public webroot is not.
> The reduction script lives with the captures in this repo
> (`datasets/screens/flows/reduce_flows.py`) and only the JSON crosses over.
> It refuses to run if a capture is unaccounted for or a job runs to ten words.

*The original proposal, kept for the record:*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Serves** | Week 7 · syllabus 5 · module B2 |
| **Why** | W7's *"a flow, drawn crudely"* has never had an artefact, and it was previously not worth proposing: one lecture item did not justify anything. **B8 changed that.** Students now build a real click-through, it is a section of the hand-in, and Criterion 2 marks whether the prototype is navigable. Three claims on it instead of one — and there is still nothing published showing what a documented flow looks like. |

**Where it lives.** Collections, not the Flows floor. The floor is shut on

**Method.** For each design system in the register, record whether it publishes a

**Checked — it is not already there.** `/collections/pattern-register/` turned out

### SD·07 — Tab, and the ring
`interfaces.institute/collections/keyboard-grammar/#tabstops` · **BUILT 2026-09-02 — not in the original plan**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Serves** | Week 9 · module B3 · programme A4 |
| **Why** | §3 above listed keyboard-grammar as a free win for week 9 and that was a mistake. The 27 keys are the keys you press *after* focus has arrived. Tab is not among them and never will be — the Authoring Practices describe patterns, and Tab belongs to the document. The collection was complete and silent on the thing everyone means by "keyboard navigation". |

**What it is.** A specimen modelled on a real registration form that counts
itself: **10 focusable controls, 8 tab stops.** The two differ because three
radio buttons are one stop and the arrows move inside the group — which is
`Arrow Down` and `Arrow Up` from the table above, doing the job Tab is not
doing. The rule is implemented, not described, so the count survives someone
editing the markup.

**The demonstration.** A button takes the focus ring away and puts it back.
While it is off, the readout says only *"the specimen is not saying"* — the page
still knows exactly where focus is and has simply stopped telling you, which is
the failure in one sentence. Restore the ring and it shows the path taken while
blind. Opt-in and reversible: a page that hides its own focus ring by default is
a page a keyboard user cannot read, and this one is read by exactly those people.

**In the room.** This is the lecture item verbatim — *"then tab through one where
the focus ring was removed; say nothing during the second one"* — so run it, do
not narrate it.

The four criteria are read out of the WCAG collection rather than typed. 2.1.1 is
among them on purpose: removing the ring does not fail Focus Visible alone, it
fails **Keyboard at Level A**, because a control you cannot locate is a control
you cannot operate.

### SD·08 — Two paragraphs, same design
`modules/sd/example-sheet.html#defended` · **BUILT 2026-09-02 — module page, not an institute room**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Serves** | Week 10 · Criterion 4 |
| **Why** | W10's *"two paragraphs, same design"* was the last item with nothing behind it, and it was the one that should **not** become an institute room. Describing versus defending is not a measurable fact about interfaces; it is a fact about this rubric, on this brief, with these students. The federation publishes measured evidence, and putting a writing exercise there would have been a category error. |

**What it is.** C4 of the example portfolio, described and defended, side by
side — the described half read off the C4 slide at run time so the pair cannot
drift from the portfolio it belongs to.

**Why C4 and not another section.** It is the only rationale section where the

evidence the student collected and walked past — which is the point. Describing
lets you do that; defending does not.

**The reveal.** Critical Reflection is the lowest of the four criteria here — **38,
against 50 for the visual work** — and the marker's reason is one word:
*descriptive*. Not one colour changed between the two paragraphs. The described
one spent 40 of its 90 words; the defended one spent 88. **They had the same
allowance.**

The sheet throws at load if the defended paragraph exceeds the brief's word
limit. A rewrite that breaks the brief is not a rewrite, it is a longer answer.
