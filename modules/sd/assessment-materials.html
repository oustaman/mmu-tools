Marking

How the portfolio is marked. To see what earns marks, look at the Examples.

How it is marked

One overall mark for the portfolio as a whole. The four criteria are not weighted separately, so a strong prototype does not offset a rationale that evidences nothing. In the brief’s words, grades reflect overall performance across all criteria, not a mechanical average. This is the rubric we use, word for word from the brief; print it blank to mark an example, or your own deck.

The one thing that changes your grade

In Part C, every section quotes a number you wrote in Part A. That is the difference between a pass and a good mark, and it takes no extra work: the numbers are already in your spec sheet. Evidenced means something a marker can see: a number from your spec sheet, a before and an after, a contrast ratio. A claim with no number behind it is description, and description scores lower than defence.

✗  "I chose a warm palette because it feels friendly."
✓  "I changed body text from #8B8382 to #56504F because the first pair was 2.9:1 and failed AA."

Your grid, type and colours don’t change your mark; contrast can

No grid, typeface or colour earns marks, and none loses them: a marker will not prefer a serif to a sans, or blue to orange. What is marked is that you decided, wrote it down in A3–A5, used it the same way on every screen, and said why in C2–C4. The one exception is contrast: text below 4.5:1 against its background fails WCAG AA, and that can lose you marks. Ready-made, checked options are in the Gallery.

The marks we can give

Every mark, each criterion and the overall, is one of these steps. There is no 66: a portfolio between the two is a 65 or a 68.
