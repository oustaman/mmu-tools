Week 5 · Wireframes · B5

A wireframe is an argument about priority

Decide now: the screen’s job, what is on it, what comes first, the real words, where it goes next.

Not yet: fonts, colours, icons, photographs. Everything on the right that is not on the left was decided without asking whether the screen works.

If you are choosing a font, stop. Your week 4 decisions come back when you finish the screens.

Two ways to do it: both count for B5

A pen, or the Screen maker

Sketch, then a photo

Screen maker

  • Sketching is faster for trying several versions. A marker stops you drawing small details.
  • The Screen maker is tidier, and the words are easy to change.
  • Mix them: sketch three ideas, then build the one that works.
  • Boxes and labels either way. A box with a cross means “a picture goes here”.

The building blocks

Organisms, and a budget of 14 rows

A button alone does nothing. A question with its answers does a job. Brad Frost’s atomic design calls that an organism: small parts working as one unit. Each has a code, a name and a job.

  • Every organism is 375 px wide, and a whole number of 56 px rows, so any fits under any other.
  • A screen has 14 rows. When they run out, something has to go, or move to another screen. That limit is the point.
  • Big-screen project? Desktop has the same rows, across 12 columns.

One whole journey · the missed bin’s new version

One screen per step, one job per screen

Give each screen a job: under ten words, starting with a verb. Six screens, organisms placed, but only thirteen different ones. That repetition is a system.

      Screens 3 and 4 of 6

      The same block, doing a different job

        Using an organism again is a good sign: people only learn it once. Empty rows are fine too.

        Use the real words. “Blue bin: recycling, due today” can be wrong, so you can read it and disagree. “An answer” cannot.

        Screens 5 and 6 of 6

        Show it back, then say what happens next

        Every answer with a Change link, and a button that says what it does. Then the screen the old form never showed: a reference, a date, and what to do if it does not happen.

          Today

          Your turn: answer your worst ✗

          1. Which ✗ on your flow costs people the most?
          2. What two or three screens would answer it? Give each a job.
          3. Wireframe them, sketched or in the Screen maker. Quick rather than tidy.
          4. The blind test: hand them to a partner, in order. Can they say what each one is for, and what they would do next? Redraw any they cannot.
          5. Put them in B5. That is the start of your set.
