What a wireframe is for

A wireframe is where you decide what goes on a screen, in what order, and in what words.

Leave fonts, colours, icons and photos for later. Your choices from week 4 come back when you finish the screens.

Both count for B5

The building blocks

Organisms

375 px wide. 56 px rows. 14 rows to a screen.

Each organism does one job, like a question with its answers.

When you run out of rows, something has to go or move to another screen. On desktop you get the same rows across 12 columns.

The missed bin’s new version

One whole journey

There’s one screen for each step. Try describing each screen’s job in under ten words. It uses organisms across six screens, but only thirteen different ones.

Screen 1 of 6

The start

The old page had 199 words. The deadline is the only rule that decides whether it’s worth reporting, so that’s the one we show.

    “First use” marks an organism this journey hasn’t used yet. On the screens that follow, you’ll see how few new ones each screen needs.

    Screen 2 of 6

    Address first

    Once the council knows the address, it knows which bins were due. That’s why this comes first.

      Screens 3 and 4 of 6

      The same organism twice

        Both screens use the same block for different questions, so people only have to learn it once.

        Use real words from the start. You can read “Blue bin: recycling, due today” and disagree with it, which you can’t do with “An answer”.

        Screens 5 and 6 of 6

        Checking, and finishing

        People can change any answer before sending it. The last screen gives them a reference, a date, and what to do if nothing happens. The old form had no screen like this.

          In the Screen maker

          1. Click an organism to add it, or drag it onto a row.
          2. Click any words to change them.
          3. Drag a piece to move it. Rows can stay empty.
          4. + New screen gives you a blank screen or one of the twenty kinds.
          5. Anything every screen shares, like the top bar, goes on the master.
          • Copy each screen into your deck as a picture, or print the set.
          • Annotate adds numbered notes around a screen, and they copy with the picture.
          • If nothing fits, use a note to say what would go there.
          • On a university computer, press Save and keep the text before you log off.

          Your wireframe

          No app or website yet?

          Use whatever people use now

          A message thread, a paper form, a diary or a spreadsheet works the same way. Ask the owner first, and blur everyone else’s details.

          1. 146 hours before anyone answers
          2. 2The price list is a tilted photo, with the 8-inch price cut off
          3. 3Saturday has filled up while it waited

          A phone call is still a step on your flow, but pick something you can draw for this part. Nothing to capture? Ask someone how they manage today and recreate it with made-up names, saying so in the caption.

          Your turn

          1. Map the journey as it is today, and mark each ✗.
          2. Screenshot or photograph every step for D1.
          3. Pick the ✗ that costs people the most, and wireframe that screen just as it is.
          4. Number what goes wrong, and put it beside the screenshot.
          5. Duplicate your wireframe, clear the numbers, and change it until the ✗ has gone. That’s your first screen for B5.
