What are we fixing?
  • By eye — eleven different gaps and no two boxes lined up. It looks “nearly fine” on its own; it is why the next screen never matches. A3, C2
  • Something for everyone — a typeface for every mood and a size for every line, so nothing is more important than anything else. Real small-business sites look like this. A4, C3
  • Brand first — pale grey text at 3.0:1, white on lemon at 1.4:1, and a green that only says “success” to people who can see green. A5, C4, A6
  • Every one of these is a choice someone made on purpose. The fix is not talent: it is deciding once, writing it down, and using it everywhere.

Pick a grid, a type pairing and a palette from published design systems. The same three screens redraw with whatever you pick — one set of decisions, applied to every screen. That is what A3, A4 and A5 are for: hand them to someone else and the next screen they build will match.

Every option says where it comes from, so no AI is needed: cite the source in C9 and say in C3 and C4 why you chose it, and what you changed. Type your own words below and the screens become your project — screenshot them for your style tile. Further down: the same screen at four levels of finish, and which one your finished screens need.

The same screen, four ways

Your own first screen, drawn at four levels of finish. Every level uses the same grid, the same sizes and the same order — only how much is shown changes. Your finished screens (B6) can be wireframes — as long as your style tile (B3) shows your type and colour on real components, so a marker can see them. Level 3, “applied spec”, is the easiest way to show every decision in one place. Anything beyond it earns nothing.

“Polished” and “production-quality” in the rubric mean consistent (every screen from the same numbers), complete (every screen of the journey, states included) and working (every link clicks) — not decorated.
