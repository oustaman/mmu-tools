Buying a 16–25 Railcard

16-25railcard.co.uk, “the official retailer of Railcards by National Rail” · walked on 22 October 2026 on a phone, as far as the terms-and-conditions box — the walk stopped there, without accepting them · the steps after it come from the site’s own help pages, and are marked as such · the screens are redrawn, because the site licenses nothing · see the live site ↗

WhoA 19-year-old student who has just been told a Railcard pays for itself in three trips home.
GoalBuy one, on a phone, and have it in time for Friday’s train.
Why this caseA well-made site with a confident look — and a journey that keeps its hardest requirements until after you have started.

Now · screen 4 of 6

The question that changes everything comes last

  1. 7A promotional code box before the main questionMost buyers have no code. An empty box with a grey button invites a search for one.
  2. 8“Who is it for?” is asked after the priceIf it is for someone else, it is their age, ID and photo that count — but by now the duration has been chosen against the buyer’s.

    What a marker would credit, and where it goes in your deck

    The order of questions is arguedWho it is for first, because it decides the rest.
    Honest about what was walkedThe walk stopped at the terms box; the later steps are taken from the help pages, named and dated.
    Brand kept, contrast fixedThe orange is darkened until it passes, not replaced.
    Requirements said earlyThe ID and photo appear before anyone commits.
    A specific ethics pointPersuasion by popularity replaced with a fact; terms summarised where they are agreed to.
    Its biggest assumption is written downOne sign-in for website and app. If that is not possible, the last screen changes — and the case says so.

    The Railcard pages are redrawn for criticism and review. The improved screens are a teaching example, not an official Railcard design; the Railcard number and dates on them are invented.
