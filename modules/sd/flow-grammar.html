Drawing a flow

Week 5 · The journey · A2, B4

A flow is the order a person meets things

Not a sitemap, which shows how an organisation stores its pages. A flow shows what one person does, one thing after another, to get one thing done. It has a small grammar: six marks, each with one meaning.

Move with ← →; Present for the projector.

The grammar · 1 and 2 of 6

Step: a numbered box, and where it happens

Is it a step? Would the person say “then I…”? One box per page, call or message. “The website” is too big, “click Next” too small.

The small line is a cost you can count. The bakery takes four channels to place one order, and the first three cannot take it.

The grammar · 3 of 6

Wait: time when the person can do nothing

It gets no number, because there is nothing to do. But it is counted, because it is often the longest part of the journey. The bakery’s first reply takes 46 hours, and by then Saturday is fully booked.

The grammar · 4 of 6

✗ Stuck: where people go wrong

The most important mark on the flow: these are the problems your design has to answer.

Write a fact, not an adjective. “199 words before you can start”, not “too much text”.

The grammar · 5 and 6 of 6

Diamond: the answer sends people different ways

Is it a diamond? Only if the answer changes where the person goes next. “Which bin was not emptied?” ends in a question mark, but every answer leads to the same screen, so it is a step.

The arrow back: will anything be different the second time round? If not, it is a trap.
