Week 5 · A2, B4

What a flow is

A flow shows what one person does, step by step, to get one thing done. There are six marks to learn.

Marks 1 and 2 of 6

Step, and where it happens

Each step gets a numbered box, with where it happens underneath. Make one box for each page, call or message.

You’ll also hear the word touchpoint. It means any moment a person comes into contact with a service, like the bakery’s website, menu, phone and messages.

Mark 3 of 6

Wait

A wait doesn’t get a number, since the person can’t do anything, but do count it. The bakery’s first reply took 46 hours.

Mark 4 of 6

✗ Stuck

Mark the places where people go wrong. These are the problems your design has to fix, so write down what actually happens, like “199 words before you can start”.

Marks 5 and 6 of 6

Diamond and arrow back

Use a diamond when the answer changes where someone goes next. “Which bin was not emptied?” is a step, since every answer leads to the same screen.

If “no” sends people back, ask whether anything will be different the second time.

Beyond the six marks

Branches that join up

A question can have any number of answers, each with its own name. A branch can stop, or join the main path again, as these two do at Sent.

Pills mark the start and the end, and only your own screens get a number. Colours mean what you decide, so add a key: here, blue is a screen and green is the end.
