The rest of the organisms

This is the bakery’s new version. Compare it with the missed bin and you’ll see the top bar, title, notice and button are the same, and only the middle changes. There’s no need to learn the rest of the set. Just look through it once so you know what’s there.

Screen 3 of 6

Big blocks

A date picker takes six of the fourteen rows. That’s fine here, since choosing the day is the whole point of this screen.

    What does the person need to do?

    Choose, or give you something

    Use round marks for one answer and square for several. Ask for as little as you can, and if you chose not to collect something, say so in C6.

    What does the person need to do?

    Find something, or get around

    You only need search when there are too many things to look through. A tab bar suits an app with several main parts, while a journey with an end usually just needs a way back and a progress bar.

    What does the person need to do?

    Look at things, or do something bigger

    Today’s main subject

    Empty, loading and failed are screens too

    For every screen on your list, ask what it looks like when it’s empty, when it’s loading and when it fails. You’ll probably find one or two more screens.

    Overlays and notes

    OVL-01 Dialog

    OVL-02 Bottom sheet

    OVL-03 Keyboard

    OVL-04 Toast

    OWN-01 to OWN-06 Your own

    Overlays sit on top of a screen and don’t take up rows, and you can only have one at a time. Keep dialogs for things that can’t be undone. If nothing in the set fits, put a note where it would go and write what it would do. You don’t need to be able to draw it.

    Desktop · 12 columns

    Bigger screens

    This is “Which cake?” on a desktop. The words are the same, but now you have to decide what goes beside what.

    Desktop adds a site header, breadcrumbs, a side menu, a table, three cards and a footer. The tab bar, app bars, bottom sheet and keyboard are phone only.

    You only need this if people use your service on a big screen.

    Your turn

    1. Go through your list of screens and ask the empty, loading and failed question for each one.
    2. Wireframe every screen, starting with the most boring one.
    3. If you’re stuck for a layout, start from one of the twenty kinds and change it.
    4. Try the blind test with a partner.

    Bring the whole set to week 8. That’s your B5, and we’ll start finishing screens from it.
