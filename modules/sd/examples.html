Examples

What earns marks, shown rather than described: four services walked before and after, then two complete portfolios, one thin and one strong. How it is marked is on its own page.

Before and after

  1. Now — every screen, with what goes wrong numbered on it.
  2. Measured — words, taps, fields, contrast: the counts behind each problem.
  3. Improved — the same job, redesigned, with each decision explained.
  4. The decisions — the redesign’s own A3, A4 and A5.
  5. Counted — before against after, and what happened to every step.
  6. Why it is better — what a marker credits, and which deck section it belongs in.
Six questions for every case

Keep these in front of you while you study a case. Write numbers, not adjectives. Most answers are on the case’s slide called The redesign’s A3, A4 and A5, near the end of each walkthrough.

  1. Who is it for, and where are they when they use it? (the Start slide)
  2. Type: how many sizes does the redesign use, and what is each one for? Why is the smallest not smaller?
  3. Colour: what is the lowest ratio any text has in its A5? Which colour is kept away from text, and what is its ratio? (The Railcard and Welsh course cases also count the text that failed 4.5:1 before.)
  4. Grid: how many columns on a phone, and how wide is the main content on a laptop? What reason is given?
  5. Kept: what did the redesign keep from the original service, and why keep it?
  6. Yours: which row would you take for your own A3, A4 or A5 — and what would you change for your audience?
