01

One corpus, eight views

The instinct here is to build a dataset per instrument — screenshots for the teardown tool, layouts for the grid playground, failures for the accessibility checklist. Don't. Those are the same objects with different fields queried.

Capture each screen once, annotate it richly, give it an id. sd-scr-047 is then simultaneously a hierarchy exhibit in week 1, a grid decomposition in week 3, a colour example in week 5, and an accessibility case in week 9 — and because it's one record, a student who clicks a principle can see the screens that demonstrate it, and a screen can show every principle it touches. That cross-linking is what makes the instruments feel like a system rather than eight unrelated pages, and it costs nothing if the ids exist from the start.

It also means the annotation effort concentrates in one place, which matters when the realistic budget is one summer.

File conventions

JSON with a metadata envelope, matching facilities/campus_rooms_data.json — dataset_name, extraction_date, source_citation per record. The provenance fields aren't bureaucracy here: for the screen corpus they're the copyright position, and for the rationale bank they're the moderation trail.

02

The datasets

screens.jsonBefore wk 1Join key~140 records · 28 hrs

Annotated captures of real interfaces. The single largest build and the one everything else references. Aim for breadth of screen type rather than breadth of brand — six checkout flows teach less than one each of onboarding, empty state, error, form, list and detail.

id · source · platform · url_or_app · captured_date · category · screen_type (onboarding / list / detail / form / empty / error / success / settings) · reading_order[] · devices_used[] (scale, weight, colour, space, position) · does_well[] · does_badly[] · principle_ids[] · a11y_failures[] · grid{} · breakpoints_captured[] · licence_note

Sizing: ~70 records covers weeks 1–5 and is the pre-term minimum. Capture 20 of them at all three breakpoints for week 9. Budget 3–4 screens an hour if you're annotating properly — the capture is fast, the reading_order and devices_used fields are where the time goes, and those are the fields that make the teardown instrument work.

themes.jsonBefore wk 1~80 records · 6 hrs

The theme bank students choose their portfolio subject from — the SD equivalent of UXCI's service-selector, and at a hundred students the thing that prevents forty identical Spotify redesigns.

id · theme · domain · why_it_works_at_L4 · screen_count_estimate · pitfalls[] · too_big_variant · example_screen_ids[]

Why 80: with 100 students you need enough options that collisions are incidental rather than systemic. The too_big_variant field earns its place — it names the version of each theme that's too large for the module, so the tool can say "not a music streaming service; one playlist-sharing feature" rather than just rejecting the idea.

principles.jsonBefore wk 1~30 records · 8 hrs

The Level 4 subset — visual design principles alongside the UCD basics, each anchored to a real example and a real counter-example from the corpus. This is the adapt of ucd-principles-library, cut down hard.

id · family (visual / ucd / accessibility) · name · one_line · plain_gloss · example_screen_id · counter_example_screen_id · common_misuse · weeks[]

The two fields that matter: plain_gloss, because the existing library is pitched at Level 6 and these students have no design vocabulary yet; and common_misuse, because it's what a tutor circulating a workshop actually needs — the misreading that's about to appear in thirty portfolios.

rationales.jsonBefore wk 148 paragraphs · 10 hrs

Banded exemplar paragraphs — six rationale sections × four grade bands × two examples each. Small file, highest leverage in the set.

section (1–6) · band (fail / pass / good / excellent) · text · why_this_band · what_moves_it_up

Do this one yourself and don't skip it. It's three things at once: the week 10 lecture material (the same paragraph written badly and well, side by side), the calibration anchor for the automated pre-check, and your moderation evidence. Without it the LLM feedback has nothing to compare against and will drift toward generic encouragement — which is worse than no feedback, because students act on it. It's also the one dataset that can't be delegated or generated, since the whole point is that the bands reflect your standard.

typefaces.jsonBy wk 3~50 + 25 pairs · 8 hrs

Google Fonts only — free, students can actually use them, and they load inside your instruments' content policy where anything else silently falls back. Plus a curated pairings file with a stated reason per pair.

family · classification · weights[] · x_height_band · best_roles[] · pairs_with[] · avoid_with[] · google_fonts_url · notes
pairings: display_family · body_family · why_it_works · what_it_suits · example_screen_id

Constraint worth stating in the tool itself: restricting to Google Fonts is a teaching decision, not just a licensing one. A bounded set makes pairing a judgement about fit rather than a search problem, which is the right difficulty at Level 4.

palettes.jsonBy wk 4~30 records · 6 hrs

Worked palettes with roles assigned and every pair's contrast ratio pre-computed. Half exemplary, half deliberately failing — the failures teach more, and you need them for the week 5 workshop.

id · name · context · swatches[{ hex, role }] · pairs[{ fg, bg, ratio, aa, aaa }] · teaching_note · deliberate_failure (bool)

The ratios compute themselves — write the WCAG relative-luminance function once and generate the pairs array from the swatches. That same function is the engine of the Contrast Lab and of the automated check that a student's declared ratios match their declared hex values, so it gets written regardless. Build it here first.

grids.jsonBy wk 2~30 records · 6 hrs

Real layouts decomposed into their underlying structure. Strictly an extension of screens.json rather than a standalone file — same ids, extra fields.

screen_id · viewport · columns · gutter · margin · max_width · spacing_base · spacing_steps[] · notes

Needed early despite being a week 3 topic — the grid playground needs specimens to demonstrate against from its first lecture, and decomposing a layout accurately is slower than it looks.

ai-samples.jsonCapture late~20 records · 5 hrs

Generated screens and style tiles with the prompt kept alongside the output and a critique attached. For the week 6 lecture.

id · prompt · tool · generated_date · output_ref · competent[] · wrong[] · student_must_fix[]

Deliberately last. This is the only dataset that gets worse with age — capture it in weeks 1–4, not over the summer, or you'll stand in a lecture theatre in November showing students what generation couldn't do six months ago while they hold a phone that does it. Date-stamp every record and plan to recapture annually.

a11y-cases.jsonBy wk 8~25 records · 4 hrs

Screens with specific, nameable accessibility failures and the fix for each. Another extension of the corpus, not a new capture effort.

screen_id · failure_type · wcag_ref · what_breaks · who_it_affects · the_fix · severity

Keep who_it_affects concrete. "Fails 1.4.3" teaches nothing at Level 4; "unreadable in sunlight, and for the ~8% of men with red-green colour blindness" is the version that changes how someone designs.

What can be handed off

Screen capture is the one genuinely delegable chunk — a GTA or PGR can capture and fill the factual fields (source, platform, screen_type, captured_date) at maybe 12 screens an hour, leaving you the interpretive fields. That splits 28 hours into roughly 6 delegated and 16 yours. themes.json drafts fast with assistance and edits down. rationales.json cannot be delegated at all, and principles.json shouldn't be — both encode your standard, which is the entire point of them.

04

Two things to settle before capturing

Copyright, and where the corpus lives

Screenshots of commercial interfaces for classroom teaching sit under fair dealing for illustration for instruction — but that depends on the audience being your students, not the open web. Your module instruments have historically been served from a public webroot, and a public gallery of 140 annotated commercial screenshots is a materially different thing from a teaching exhibit.

Put the corpus behind Moodle authentication, or keep the images there and let the public instrument reference them by id. Record source, captured_date and licence_note on every record from the first capture — retrofitting provenance across 140 records is miserable, and you'd be doing it under pressure.

Screens go stale, so decide the refresh now

A meaningful slice of a 140-screen corpus will be wrong within eighteen months — apps redesign, and a teardown of a layout that no longer exists undercuts you in the room. The captured_date field makes this manageable if the instrument surfaces it: show the capture date on every exhibit, and let a student see they're looking at something from two years ago. That's honest, it's a small build, and it turns staleness from an embarrassment into a discussion about how fast the field moves.
